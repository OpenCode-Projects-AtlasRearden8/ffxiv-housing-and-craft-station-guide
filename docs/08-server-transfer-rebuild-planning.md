# Page 8 — Server-Transfer Rebuild Planning

*A fan-made guide by Asdale Dynamics, LLC — unofficial, unaffiliated, ad-free,
no account data, no transfers (of accounts or otherwise).*

So you're moving worlds — new Free Company, new friends, new Market Board.
Housing happens to be the thing server transfers are *worst* at, because a
house is bound to a world's plot economy, and a garden is bound to time.

This page is a pre-move plan. The theme: **you cannot take the plot with you,
but you can take everything that matters.**

## 1. The honest headline first

*Hedge, prominently: transfer rules, plot availability systems, relocation
tools, and gil caps change between patches. Before you spend a single gil on a
world transfer, read the current official patch notes and system announcement
for the transfer rules that apply to your account and region.* This page gives
you the *planning method*; the official rules decide the specifics.

## 2. What is at risk on a transfer

For most non-crafters, "rebuild planning" comes down to five buckets:

| Bucket | What happens on move day | Plan for it |
|--------|--------------------------|-------------|
| The plot itself | Doesn't travel | Buy-in again on the new world (Page 1) |
| Outdoor furniture / garden | Retired handles and items stay with you; the *plot* doesn't | Inventory + storage discipline |
| Indoor furniture & fixtures | Travel with your characters mostly via storage/inventory | Pre-pack |
| Gil | Subject to in-game and transfer-related caps | Spread across retainers |
| Social ties (FC, neighbours) | Change entirely | Say goodbye; screenshot the yard |

The tricky ones are **gil** and **timing**, so we deal with those directly.

## 3. The gil plan (the part people get wrong)

- **Retainers are your wallets.** Each retainer can carry its own gil. If your
  new world's housing market is friendly (or hostile), you want your gil
  distributed across retainers so you can pay deposits without a single
  character travelling overweight in cash.
- **Know the caps before the move.** There are limits on how much gil a
  character and a retainer can each carry; there are also limits that apply to
  world transfers. *Hedge: the exact numbers live in the current official
  transfer rules — check them.* Write your own per-pocket plan like this:

```plain
GIL PLAN BEFORE TRANSFER — date: ______

Character X carries:   ______ gil
Retainer A carries:    ______ gil  (housing deposit fund)
Retainer B carries:    ______ gil  (permit + fixtures fund)
Retainer C carries:    ______ gil  (furniture shopping fund)
FC chest (if allowed): ______ gil  (nothing OR an agreed amount)

Rule: no single pocket goes over the official transfer cap, and the
housing-deposit fund is identified by name so it doesn't get spent on boots.
```

## 4. The furniture plan (pack like a mover)

1. **Stop acquiring two weeks out.** The temptation right before a transfer is
   to buy "for the new place" — the problem is *you don't know the new place
   yet*, including its plot size and interior layout. Buy nothing.
2. **Stow everything possible.** Put furniture into your character's housing
   storage or your bag/retainer inventory as early as you can. The fewer
   loose items on move day, the fewer things to drop by accident.
3. **Photograph every room.** When you land on the new world and rebuild,
   you'll want the visual reference.
4. **Save a written layout.** For each room, a short note in plain text: "bed
   against the north wall, bookshelf to its right, plant on the sill".
   Layouts don't transfer, but your notes do (they're in your head/notes
   app).
5. **Dispose of clutter before, not after.** The Market Board you're leaving
   might absorb your clutter (for sale); the new world's board might not. Sell
   heavy, boring items now; take the favourites.

## 5. The garden plan (the emotional part)

A garden is *time* as much as furniture. Before transfer day:

- **Harvest everything that's ready.** Don't let crops die on the vine for a
  plot that isn't coming with you.
- **Pick your seeds.** Seeds and crops travel in inventory. The garden patch
  does not, but the genetic work you did (the seeds you crossbred) does.
- **Say thank you to the swap circle** (Page 4). Neighbours who watered for
  months deserve a send-off, and — practically — a sudden disappearance does
  nothing for the people who planted around your patch.

## 6. A pre-transfer timeline that works

This schedule is deliberately boring. Boring survives.

```plain
TRANSFER COUNTDOWN — leave-by date: ______

T-3 weeks: read official transfer rules; decide apartment vs house budget
T-2 weeks: stop buying furniture; start stowing
T-1 week:  gil plan filled out; every pocket under caps
T-5 days:  harvest + pick garden; say goodbye in FC chat
T-2 days:  final gil distribution check; screenshot every room
Move day:  take nothing for granted; list what's in each pocket
T+1 week:  re-run Page 1 (buy-in); re-run Page 7 (move-in checklists)
T+1 month: re-plant the garden; tell the new neighbours what's up
```

## 7. The FC question

Moving worlds usually means leaving your Free Company behind. Two quick notes:

- **Leave gracefully, and before the transfer technically happens.** A quiet,
  kind exit (a short in-game or out-of-game message to your FC leader) is the
  right move, and it keeps the door open if you ever transfer back.
- **Your new FC will not know you yet.** Bring one photo of your old yard and
  a small story about the garden. Nothing opens doors in a new ward faster.

## For tomorrow

If a transfer is on your calendar, do the T-3 weeks line today: it's reading,
not spending. Decisions made calmly at T-3 are always cheaper than decisions
made in the last 48 hours.
