# Page 1 — Buy-In Budget Breakdown

*A fan-made guide by Asdale Dynamics, LLC — unofficial, unaffiliated, ad-free,
request-free. See [the disclaimer](index.html#a-very-important-disclaimer) on
the index page.*

The first rule of FFXIV housing: the **house price is not the price.** The gil you
feed the plot is the entry fee. Everything after is furniture, permits, plants,
and a chocobo stable if you know what's good for you.

This page gives you the full buy-in picture in one place. Hedge note up front,
because it matters: **plot prices in residential districts vary by plot grade,
zone, and patch.** The figures below are order-of-magnitude, the kind you plan
around, not the kind you take to the bank.

## 1. The four things you pay for

To get *into* housing as a non-crafter you will pay four times:

1. **The plot itself** — paid to the housing system (a plaque/board in the district).
2. **The housing permit** — a permit to build, bought from the in-game vendor for
   the district, priced by *house size*, not by plot.
3. **Basic fixtures** — the stuff that makes a house a house: your outside wall,
   door, and roof, and inside walls, floors, and lighting. Much of this comes in
   **wall/floor/door sets** you can buy rather than craft.
4. **First-outfit furniture** — the bed, the counter, the chairs from Page 2.

### Rough planning numbers

These are the magnitudes most players budget around. *Hedge: confirm in game
before you decide; the game prices are the truth.*

| Item | Rough order of magnitude |
|------|--------------------------|
| Free Company apartment | ~500,000 gil |
| Personal apartment | ~500,000 gil |
| Small house plot | multi-millions (roughly single-digit millions) |
| Medium house plot | roughly double-digit millions |
| Large house plot | roughly 40m–50m gil territory |
| Housing permit (small) | roughly in the hundreds of thousands of gil |
| Housing permit (medium/large) | scaling up with size |
| Basic wall/door/floor sets | modest, buyable from housing vendors |
| A chocobo stable for your yard | a few hundred thousand gil |

**The apartment is the hero that saves you.** If you are a non-crafter who
mostly wants a glamour dresser door, a bed, a flower pot, and somewhere to log
out of the rain, an apartment is *definitively* the right first purchase. It is
orders-of-magnitude cheaper than a house, it is available in every residential
district, and nobody judges you (only the FC chat does, and that's different).

## 2. What actually hits your wallet first

When you finally press "buy", here's the sequence most non-crafters go through:

- **Deposit** the plot price.
- **Permit** the moment you own the plot (you cannot build without it).
- **Exterior fixture set** (door, walls, roof) and then
- **Interior fixture set** (walls, floor, lighting — think wallpaper and carpet
  you like; this is where taste happens, and taste is free).

Add-in items people forget on the first pass:

- **Storage.** Congrats, you own furniture. Where does the *original* furniture
  you replaced go? There is an in-game housing storage; budget for remembering
  that it exists rather than for gil.
- **Outdoor furniture** (fencing, beds, that stable). All sold separately.
- **A welcome mat of zugzwang** — i.e., you will absolutely buy a rug, then want
  a rug the same colour, then wish you had asked first. Budget for one mistake.

## 3. The cheapest path in (a checklist)

A minimal, non-crafter move-in, in order:

1. **50 levels of gil discipline.** Most non-crafters accumulate their first few
   million from duty roulettes, the Market Board, and not asking questions of
   other players. It takes time; that's okay.
2. **Decide apartment vs house honestly.** If you're unsure, read Page 2 and
   Page 7 first, then decide.
3. **Check plot availability** *immediately after* the in-game housing timer, or
   look for the "On Sale" plaque. Relocation of existing plots also opens once a
   bulletin; a free plot is rare but not fictional.
4. **Buy plot + permit + fixture sets in one sitting.** Sitting in limbo with a
   plot you can't build on is genuinely miserable.
5. **Buy Page 2's "essentials" list only.** Everything else can wait a week.

## 4. What about the FC?

One note, because it saves friendships:

- **An FC house is *the FC's* house.** Rings and rights to decorate are
  controlled by the company. If you're buying in *with* people, agree in writing
  (in the FC motd or a shared note) who pays what, who decorates, and who has
  the key ring. This avoids surprises.

## For tomorrow

Look up the apartment prices in **your** residential district today, not the
house prices. Resolve to survive very well in one apartment before you dream in
six rooms.
