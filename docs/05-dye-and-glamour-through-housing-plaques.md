# Page 5 — Dye and Glamour Through Housing Plaques

*A fan-made guide by Asdale Dynamics, LLC — unofficial, unaffiliated, ad-free,
no-logins, no-drama.*

Here is the best-kept secret in housing for people who never craft: **you can
be fabulous without ever opening a crafting menu.** Dyes, glamours, mannequins,
and the little name plaques by your door are all purchasable, droppable, or
vendor-bought. This page walks through the whole "looking good" stack.

## 1. The plaques, first (they're tiny, they matter)

A few housing items are flat "plaque"-style name and message boards that hang
near your door or plot boundary:

- Your **housing placard** — the plot sign showing who lives there.
- **Message plaques / wall-mounted signs** you place as furnishings, where
  people can leave notes for visitors or label rooms.

Two non-crafter specific tips:

- **Plaques can match your dye scheme.** If you have a colour story (see §3),
  pick plaques whose default wood tone sits near the colours you're using on
  the walls. Visual cohesion for zero effort.
- **Plaques are the world's cheapest "readable housing".** A tiny sign that
  says "brews are free; raid nothing" does more for a visitor than any
  expensive tapestry.

## 2. Dyes: the whole loop

Dyes let you recolour gear (and, in some cases, certain housing items). For a
non-crafter:

1. **Get a dye.** The cheapest and most reliable sources: your Grand Company
   quartermaster, city vendors, the Market Board, and event rewards. *Hedge:
   availability rotates; check the Market Board search for "General Purpose
   Dye"* to see what's cheap this week.
2. **Open your Character menu**, select the piece of gear, and choose the dye
   option from the menu that appears (exact menu wording shifts between
   patches, but it's always reachable from the gear slot itself).
3. **Pick the dye.** One dye bottle covers one gear piece.
4. **Wear the new colour** with pride, or park the piece on a mannequin.

Dye tips for non-crafters:

- **Dyes are consumable and cheap at the bottom end.** The common dyes are
  pocket change. Do experiment freely at home.
- **Rare dyes are an investment, not an impulse.** Before spending real gil on
  a rare dye, try a similarly-toned cheap dye on the same gear first. Some
  colours only truly work on certain textures.
- **Housing can be dyed too.** Some housing items and wall treatments accept
  dye/paint via the housing menu's "tint" or "dye" option. *Hedge:* which
  items accept dye varies by item and patch; the test is whether the housing
  menu shows the dye option for that item.

## 3. Pick a colour story, not a colour

New homeowners rarely regret choosing *matching* furniture. What they regret is
choosing forty colours at once.

A simple non-crafter method:

1. Pick **one** colour you love (this is your "hero" colour).
2. Pick **one neutral** that goes with everything (bone, grey, dark wood).
3. Buy dyes and furniture in roughly a **70/30 split** between neutral and hero.

This is the same trick interior designers use in the real world, translated
into FFXIV dye bottles, and it will make your house look intentional even if
every item in it came from a vendor.

## 4. Glamour: looking good in borrowed clothes

"Glamour" is the game's transmog system: make actual wearable gear *look like*
other gear you own, without changing any stats. Non-crafters can do all of it.

The short version:

- **The glamour dresser** lives in inn rooms (and similar lodging). Deposit
  cosmetic gear you own into it, and build **glamour plates** — presets that
  apply several pieces at once.
- **Mannequins** are housing furnishings you "dress" with a set of gear. They
  display it in your room like a tailor's dummy. Friends can view them;
  mannequins are also how you keep "I want to look like that one day" sets
  physically present instead of buried in an armoury chest.
- **Glamour plates won't survive every change** — if you redesign a plate,
  test it once before applying it to your whole wardrobe.

Practical glamour storage advice:

- **Deposit liberally, keep the dresser tidy.** Rename plates and delete old
  ones you never use. A tidy glamour dresser is a gift to future-you.
- **Don't hoard near-duplicates.** If two pieces look the same undyed, keep
  the one you like best, and let the other go.

## 5. A complete "look-good" shopping list for non-crafters

| Item | Rough role | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| Common dyes | Pocket-change recolours | Buy several, experiment freely |
| Rare dyes | Precision recolours for key gear | Test a cheap dye similar in tone first |
| One mannequin | Show off one "future set" | More later, only if useful |
| Plaque / sign furnishings | Labelling + charm | Cheapest personality per gil |
| Matching wallpaper & floor | The cohesive backdrop | Do this *before* dyeing everything |
| A plate of glamours | One good everyday look | Build it once, reuse forever |

## 6. Repair-adjacent note

Dyed gear, like all gear, degrades and needs repairs (Page 6 covers the log).
Dyeing does not affect condition, so there's no downside — dye early, dye
often, but check gear condition after long crafting sessions in the field.

## For tomorrow

Try one cheap dye on one piece of gear you'll keep for a while. If you like it,
dye the whole set the same way and see whether a *set* in one colour suddenly
looks like a real outfit — it usually does.
