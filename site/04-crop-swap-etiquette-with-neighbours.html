Page 4 — Crop-Swap Etiquette with Neighbours

A fan-made guide by Asdale Dynamics, LLC — unofficial, unaffiliated, ad-free, and it will never ask you to log in to anything.

In FFXIV, a garden is only partly yours. Anyone who can enter your yard can water your plants. Ward means neighbourhood, and neighbourhood means people — and people, in gardens, is where etiquette comes from.

This page is about the informal economy that grows up between neighbours who water each other's plants and swap crops and seeds. None of it is enforced by the game; all of it is enforced by your reputation.

1. The gardener's compact (the unwritten rules)

These are the norms that have grown up in housing wards over the years. They are conventions, not laws — different FCs and wards run slightly different house rules — but if you follow them, you will be known as a good neighbour.

  1. Water even when you don't need to. Watering a neighbour's garden costs you nothing and helps them. If you see a wilting garden and know the owner isn't in, water it. Everyone does this for everyone.
  2. Ask before taking seeds. If crossbreeding produced new seeds in a neighbour's patch — or if you're harvesting near — the friendly move is to check in chat before taking anything that isn't clearly your harvest.
  3. Share the surplus, keep your promise. If you told the FC you'd bring seeds to the Thursday water-haul, bring seeds to the Thursday water-haul.
  4. Don't touch someone's harvest out of politeness unless the owner said "harvest freely". Opinions differ; default to asking.
  5. Say thanks in say-chat. A literal "thanks for the water!" goes further than you'd think. Housing is a chat economy as much as a garden one.

2. What a crop swap actually is

A crop swap is when a small group of neighbours or FC-mates agree, in advance, to each plant a different seed, then trade part of their harvest so everybody gets variety, and everyone's crossbreeding improves.

It works because of two mechanics working together:

  • Crossbreeding rewards diversity: different, compatible seeds planted in adjacent beds produce new, more valuable seeds at harvest time.
  • Markets punish sameness: if everyone grows the same crop, the price of that crop craters on your server. A swap means you're always diversified.

A typical round looks like this:

  1. A neighbour says in FC chat: "starting a swap, three slots, water daily".
  2. Each participant plants a different seed in their own patch.
  3. Everyone waters each other's gardens when they can (the compact, above).
  4. At harvest, each person keeps some of their own crop and trades the rest based on whatever Market prices make a fair relist.

3. The etiquette FAQ

Q: Can other players steal my crops? Hedge: the rules around who can interact with a garden (outside your own character's controls) have changed between patches; treat your patch's visibility settings in game as the source of truth. In practice, though, "stealing" is rare — most garden theft is actually a misunderstanding.

Q: Someone took seeds from my garden without asking. What do I do? Ask politely in say-chat first. Ninety-nine times out of a hundred it's confusion about who harvested what, and it's fixed with one message. Resentment that simmers silently is exactly how housing wards go bad.

Q: Should you charge for watering? No. Watering isn't a service; it's the social glue. If a neighbour offers gil, take it only if it changes your behaviour. Otherwise, decline and water another garden.

Q: Do I have to join the FC's swap? No. Politely pass when you want to; say "I'm doing my own landscaping this cycle," and that's a complete answer. A polite pass is far better than a grudging yes you don't water through.

4. A swap charter you can paste into your FC

Here is a short charter you can copy into your Free Company message of the day (or a shared document) when you organise a swap. Edit freely.

NEIGHBOURHOOD SWAP CHARTER — Week of ____

1. By harvest day, each participant trades at least one crop or seed.
2. Water everything you walk past. It's free; it's the norm.
3. Seeds from crossbreeding: the taker asks the patch owner in chat first.
4. Missed waterings: tell the group, don't just disappear.
5. Listing surplus on the Market Board? Mention it — some of us keep a
   shared price floor together.
Sign your name in chat if you're in. Newcomers always welcome.

The charter exists not to police, but to keep expectations aligned — so the neighbour who under-waters doesn't quietly become the neighbour nobody tells when a rare seed comes back in stock.

5. What to do when things do go wrong

  • You under-watered and a neighbour's crop suffered. Apologise in chat, specifically, once. Offer a replacement seed or crop. Then do better.
  • A neighbour is rude about your garden. Some people are rude about everything. Water less with them.
  • Someone repeatedly ignores the compact and takes without asking. The remedy is social: you simply stop doing favours for them. It's the only remedy, and it works.

For tomorrow

Say hello to one neighbour whose garden you water this week. Gardens are only about plants when nobody's watching.
