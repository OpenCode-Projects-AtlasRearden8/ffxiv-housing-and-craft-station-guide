Page 3 — Garden Planter Math

A fan-made guide by Asdale Dynamics, LLC — unofficial, unaffiliated, ad-free, no account requests, no trackers.

Gardening is the part of housing that looks most like homework: beds, seeds, watering, and charts. This page does the homework for you, with a calculator you can rerun any time.

1. First, a very important hedge

We will say this once, in bold, and then stop:

Bed counts, grow times, and watering rules depend on which patch you own and on the current state of the game itself. The math below uses a variable N for the number of beds in your patch, and a worked example for a patch with 8 beds. Whatever your patch's real bed count is — read the item description in game, or count the plantable spots yourself — the calculator still works. If a mechanic has changed since this was written, the game wins.

2. The garden lifecycle (plain English)

For an outdoor patch in a housing yard:

  1. Buy a garden patch (an outdoor furnishing) and place it in your yard.
  2. Buy seeds from the Market Board or a vendor, or receive them from a neighbour (Page 4 is entirely about that social contract).
  3. Plant one seed per bed.
  4. Water — roughly once per real-world day. Other players can water your garden too (that's the etiquette economy of Page 4).
  5. Wait. Grow times vary by crop; many common crops take a few days.
  6. Harvest. You get the crop — and you may also get a new seed, thanks to crossbreeding, which happens when a seed is planted next to a different, compatible seed.

3. Indoor planters: the low-effort cousin

An indoor planter (a furnishing you place inside your home) lets you grow one thing at a time, per planter.

  • What it's for: a single seed at a time — usually flowers and simple projects. Check what your seed grows into before committing.
  • What it can't do: crossbreeding. Crossbred seeds come from adjacent outdoor beds, not indoor pots.
  • Why non-crafters have one: a pot of flowers on a windowsill costs little gil, takes little thought, and is the single cosiest item in the game.

4. The garden economics calculator

The whole question in one formula:

Cost of the project   = patch_price + (N × seed_price) + extras
Revenue from crops    = N × crop_value
Revenue from seeds    = new_seed_count × new_seed_value
Profit                = all revenue − all cost

Where:

  • N = number of beds in your patch.
  • crop_value = what one harvested crop sells for on the Market Board.
  • new_seed_count = how many extra seeds crossbreeding produced. This is genuinely variable — gardeners rarely get the same count twice.

Worked example — an 8-bed patch

Friendly placeholder numbers (the method matters more than the gil; your server's Market Board will differ):

  • Patch price: ~40,000 gil (one-time)
  • Seed price: ~8,000 gil each → 8 beds need 8 seeds → 64,000 gil
  • Crop value per bed at harvest (say, a few days later): ~18,000 gil
Cost   = 40,000 + (8 × 8,000) = 104,000 gil
Return = 8 × 18,000           = 144,000 gil
Profit from crops alone       =  40,000 gil
+ any crossbred seeds         = extra upside

Two harvests later you've paid off the patch and you're in real profit — and that is before counting crossbred seeds, which is where gardens can print real money on a server where a rare seed is in demand.

The big caveat: Market prices swing. If everybody on your server grew the same crop last week, your 18k crop might sell for 6k. The classic protection is Page 4's crop-swap circle: diversify by trading with neighbours who planted differently.

5. Rules of thumb

  • Water every day. Missed waterings slow crops down, and can annoy the neighbours who were counting on them (Page 4).
  • Don't fill a whole patch with one seed type unless you're sure — a single seed type everywhere usually means no crossbreeding at all.
  • One seed per bed. Beds don't share.
  • Stagger planting. If you plant everything on the same day, you harvest everything on the same day, at the same Market price. Staggering by a day or two smooths your income.
  • Don't dismiss the "pretty" seeds. Some rare crossbred flowers are worth a lot to glamour and decorating players; decoration can be a business.

For tomorrow

Count your beds (N), price one seed, price one crop, and fill in the calculator with your own real numbers. That's the whole homework.
