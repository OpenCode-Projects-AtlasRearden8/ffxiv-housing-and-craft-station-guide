Page 2 — Essential Furniture vs Clutter

A fan-made guide by Asdale Dynamics, LLC — unofficial, unaffiliated, ad-free, and it will never ask for your account info.

Every new homeowner in FFXIV goes through the same arc: you walk into the Market Board of the residential district, you see literally thousands of furniture entries, you buy forty of them, and six months later something called a "Cactuard Dining Set" lives in storage forever.

This page is the arc-stopper: the short list to buy first, and the long list to buy never (or at least, not until the first list is done).

1. The essentials (buy these first)

Treat this as the non-crafter's starter kit. Most of it is buyable from the housing vendors in your district or from the Market Board; none of it requires you to ever open a crafting menu.

Inside the house

  1. A bed. Any bed. If guests can sleep in it, better.
  2. A chair or stool, and a table. The non-crafter's formal dinner-per-plate.
  3. A lighting fixture (ceiling light or standing lamp) and interior wallpaper + flooring you actually like. This is the "it feels like a home" tier. Taste is free; buy the set that matches.
  4. A counter / stall for the kitchen wall, if you want that cozy look.
  5. A window if your wallpaper doesn't have one built in. Non-negotiable.
  6. An indoor planter (see Page 3) so you can grow indoor flowers without ever being outside in the rain.

Practical-but-boring, worth real gil

  1. A retainer bell. It summons your retainers from inside your own home. This will save you more real-world time than any other purchase.
  2. Mannequins (Page 5) for showing off glamour, if you go in for that.
  3. A housing storage approach, i.e., knowing that extra furniture can be stowed and retrieved rather than being left on the floor as "scenery".

Outside the house

  1. Fencing — even cheap fencing makes the yard read as "a yard".
  2. A garden patch or planter equivalent if you plan to garden (Page 3).
  3. A chocobo stable if you've got a chocobo and the gil; it keeps your bird feedable at home and can house the bird while you fly around as a normal person.
  4. A welcome sign or placard by the door (Page 5 covers placards too).

2. The anti-essentials (skip these until later — or forever)

This list is affectionate. Someone loves all of these. You don't have to.

  • Giant statement pieces for rooms you haven't furnished yet. It's beautiful, and it lives in storage for eight months.
  • The matching-four-of-everything sets. Sets are lovely when a room calls for them and dead weight when they don't.
  • Countertop clutter: tiny barrels, tiny posters, a dozen small plates. Charming at eight items, overwhelming at eighty.
  • Everything festival-limited at release time. Fun, thematic, and the reason your storage is at cap. Buy one, not twelve.
  • The very expensive "investment" furniture. Unless you actually host Roleplay events, the 240-apiece chairs are a vibe, not a plan.

3. A rule of thumb for clutter

For every new item, ask two questions:

  • "Does it have a place I can name?" ("It goes on the desk near the door.")
  • "Would I notice if another player rented my house and sat down?"

If the answer to the first is vague ("somewhere upstairs") and the second is no, it's clutter. Put it in storage, or leave it on the Market Board, and let future-you — who will one day have a fully decorated main room — make the call.

4. Where to buy these things (non-crafter path)

Item type Where a non-crafter usually gets it
Beds, tables, lamps, counters, windows District housing vendor; Market Board
Wallpaper, flooring, lighting Housing vendors; Market Board
Fencing, outdoor beds, stables Outdoor housing vendor in the district
Indoor planters Market Board, housing vendors
Retainer bell Housing vendor
Mannequins Housing vendors; Market Board
Fancy/festival pieces Seasonal events, vendor stock rotation, Market Board resale

Hedge: vendor stock and prices rotate with patches. If something here isn't where we say it is, a Market Board search usually finds it — try searching for the item name first and the "Housing" category second.

For tomorrow

Pick exactly five items from the essentials list. Go buy just those five. Leave the rest for next week. This is how houses get furnished without how bank accounts get emptied.
